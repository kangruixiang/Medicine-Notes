# management of tracheostomy

- related: [[Pulmonary Diseases]]
- tags: #literature
---
- [[anatomy and types of tracheostomies]]
- passy muir
	- no cuff leak: don't use. Might not be able to exhale
	- drop cuff to eat so that esophagus can expand
- trach
	- if able to, leave cuff down forever

![[2026-10-08_13-25-14.png]]
- single lumen less irritation, easier air flow, may be easier to liberate
- double lumen: removable, easier to clear secretions, can clean or replace afterwards
- double lumen generally considered safer to use because can remove to clean.
![[2026-10-08_13-26-35.png]]
- cuffless trach: moving towards speaking. Reduced risk of trauma from balloon
- cuffed trach: can be used more with mechanical ventilation. Can also reduce aspiration
- goal pressure: 18-25. Deflate cuff when able.
- speaking valve: able to tolerate deflation, minimal secretion, alert, medically stable, no upper airway obstruction
- fenestrated tube will also allow air to flow upwards for phonation
- 
![[2026-10-08_13-34-17.png]]
- fenestration can irritate airway. Not used very often.
- swallowing: deflate cuff to avoid exertion of pressure on esophagus
