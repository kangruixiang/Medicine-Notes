## Trigger Sensitivity

- determines when EPAP is changed to IPAP
- increasing the trigger sensitivity makes it easier to start a new breath
- increase the trigger sensitivity by lowering the flow needed
	- e.g. 1L is a higher trigger sensitivity than 2L and easier to start new breath

## Trise

- Use short Trise for COPD and long Trise for NMD (takes longer for diaphragm to move)
	- COPD: 100ms. Very short Trise time can cause laryngospasm and air to come out and turbulent flow
	- NMD: 600ms. Use longer Trise, otherwise they can feel overwhelmed by flow [^1]

## Ti

- total time spent in inhalation cycle
- avoid Trise higher than Ti
- only for timed modes and not for spontaneous breath
- COPD: shorten rise time can feel they can get the breath quicker

## Cycle Sensitivity

- determines when switched from IPAP to EPAP
- higher cycle sensitivity = breath cut off sooner
- increase in sensitivity makes it easier to switch. Good for COPD patients
- decrease sensitivity makes it harder to switch. Better for NMD patients.

## I to E ratio

- COPD: target 1:3
- NMD: target 1:1, sometimes I>E

## Leaks

- Target less than 50L
- 25L better
